Formula

Annual value = monthly storage fee × 12 + weekly search minutes ÷ 60 × 52 × hourly value. Monthly average = annual value ÷ 12. Cleanup payback = cleanup hours × hourly value ÷ monthly average.

Worked example

Monthly storage 68, 45 search minutes per week, hourly value 80, cleanup 6 hours: storage = 68 × 12 = 816.00; search time = 45 ÷ 60 × 52 = 39 hours per year, valued at 39 × 80 = 3,120.00; annual cost = 816.00 + 3,120.00 = 3,936.00. Cleanup value = 6 × 80 = 480.00; monthly average = 3,936.00 ÷ 12 = 328.00; payback = 480.00 ÷ 328.00 = 1.46 months. These are example inputs — your hourly value is a judgement, not a market rate.

Scope and limitations

Search time is an opportunity cost, and the model does not prove cleanup will eliminate it: a cleanup may reduce search time without removing it, and the payback figure assumes the full monthly average is recovered. Storage fees are cash costs; search-time value is not cash unless the freed time is actually used productively. Your hourly value should reflect what your time is worth to you, not a wage claim.

FAQ

How is this calculated?

Annual value = monthly storage fee × 12 + weekly search minutes ÷ 60 × 52 × hourly value. Cleanup payback divides cleanup time value by the monthly average.

Is the search-time figure real money?

No. It is an opportunity cost: the value of time spent searching, at the hourly figure you enter. It only becomes cash if you use the freed time to earn or save.

What hourly value should I enter?

Whatever your time is worth to you for this decision — it is a judgement input, not a wage or market rate. Try a low and a high value to see how much the answer moves.

Will cleanup really pay back in the months shown?

Only if cleanup removes the search time it is credited with. The model does not prove cleanup will eliminate search time; treat payback as an if-it-works figure.

How do I estimate my weekly search minutes?

Track a typical week honestly: hunting for files, photos, emails and passwords. A rough diary for one week beats a guessed round number.

Does CalcMintly store my inputs?

No. This calculation runs locally in your browser and the values you enter are not sent to a CalcMintly database.
