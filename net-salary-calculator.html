Formula

Estimated tax = gross pay × effective tax rate. Net pay = gross pay − estimated tax − social contributions − other deductions. Net pay per year = net pay per month × 12.

Worked example

Gross pay $6,000 per month, effective tax rate 15%, social contributions $500, other deductions $0: estimated tax = $6,000 × 0.15 = $900, net pay = $6,000 − $900 − $500 − $0 = $4,600 per month, or $55,200 per year.

Scope and limitations

Tax systems vary by location and personal circumstances: brackets, allowances, credits, employer contributions, and benefits are not modeled. Enter an effective rate from your own payslip or payroll calculation and verify important figures against an official payroll calculation. This is an estimate, not a payroll or tax filing.

FAQ

How does the net salary calculator work?

Net pay = gross pay − estimated tax − social contributions − other deductions, where estimated tax = gross pay × the effective tax rate you enter.

What effective tax rate should I enter?

Your actual tax divided by your gross pay from a recent payslip, or a rate your payroll department confirms. No country or region's rate is pre-filled, because statutory rates and brackets differ too much to assume.

Why is net pay negative in my calculation?

Deductions exceed gross pay at the values entered. Check the rate and deduction amounts — a negative result flags an input problem, not a real payslip.

Are employer contributions included?

No. Only deductions from your gross pay are counted. Employer-side contributions are a separate cost and do not reduce your net pay here.

Does this handle bonuses or overtime?

Only if you fold them into the monthly gross pay you enter. Irregular pay should be averaged or calculated for the specific month.

Can I use this for tax filing?

No. It is a take-home estimate. Filing depends on your jurisdiction's full rules, allowances, and credits.
