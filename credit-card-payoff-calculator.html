Principal vs interest

Bars are scaled to the total paid. At card rates the interest slice is large even for a moderate balance, which is why raising the payment helps so much.

Balance by year

AfterBalance left

Interest each month is opening balance times APR divided by 12; the payment then reduces the balance. The final payment is capped at what remains.

Formula

Each month: interest = opening balance × APR ÷ 12; ending balance = opening balance + interest − payment. The simulation repeats until the balance reaches zero.

Worked example

Balance $10,000 at 18% APR with $300 a month: the first month's interest is $150.00, and the card pays off in 47 months with $3,967.21 of interest, for $13,967.21 total.

Scope and limitations

Fixed APR, fixed monthly payment, no new purchases, no fees, no penalty rates, and no minimum-payment rules. Real cards vary the APR, charge fees, and often set the minimum as a percentage of the balance, so this is a fixed-payment estimate, not a card statement forecast.

FAQ

What if my payment is less than the monthly interest?

The balance grows and the card never pays off at that payment. The calculator flags this: raise the payment above the first month's interest figure.

Why is the interest so high compared with a loan?

Because the APR is high and the balance falls slowly at first. Most of the early payments cover interest, not principal.

Does paying more than the minimum help?

Yes, disproportionately: a higher payment both cuts the balance faster and shortens the period over which interest accrues.

What about new purchases?

Not included. New purchases add to the balance and extend the payoff time; this calculator assumes none.

Is the APR the same as the interest rate?

APR is the yearly rate used here, including the card's stated rate. Fees are separate and are not modeled.
