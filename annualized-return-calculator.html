The path applies the annualized rate evenly each year. It is a smoothed view, not a record of what actually happened year by year.

Formula

Annualized return = (final value ÷ initial value)1 ÷ years − 1. Total return = (final value − initial value) ÷ initial value.

Worked example

Initial value $10,000 growing to $15,000 over 4 years: total gain $5,000, total return 50.00%, and annualized return (15,000 ÷ 10,000)1/4 − 1 = 10.67%. That is the steady yearly rate that would carry $10,000 to $15,000 in 4 years.

Scope and limitations

This is a smoothed, backward-looking rate: it assumes growth compounds evenly and says nothing about volatility, fees, taxes, inflation, or cash added or withdrawn during the period. It cannot be compared directly with a simple average yearly return, and it is not a forecast.

FAQ

Is annualized return the same as average yearly return?

No. A simple average of yearly returns ignores compounding. The annualized (compound) rate is the single steady rate that reproduces the total growth over the period.

What if the final value is lower than the initial value?

The result is negative, which is correct: a negative annualized rate means the value shrank at that steady yearly pace.

Can I use it for periods shorter than a year?

Yes, with years as a fraction (for example 0.5 for six months), but annualizing a short period magnifies short-term moves, so treat the result with caution.

Does it include dividends or fees?

Only if they are already reflected in the final value you enter. Dividends reinvested raise the final value; fees paid out lower it.

Why is it lower than the total return divided by years?

Because compounding does part of the work: 50% over 4 years is 10.67% compounded, not 12.50% a year, since each year's growth also earns growth in later years.
