Formula

Add: a/b + c/d = (ad + bc) / bd. Subtract: a/b − c/d = (ad − bc) / bd. Multiply: a/b × c/d = ac / bd. Divide: a/b ÷ c/d = ad / bc. In every case, divide the resulting numerator and denominator by their greatest common divisor (GCD) to simplify, keeping any minus sign in the numerator.

Worked example

1/2 + 1/3 = (1×3 + 1×2) / (2×3) = 5/6. The GCD of 5 and 6 is 1, so 5/6 is already simplest, and its decimal value is 0.833333… (shown as 0.8333). Use Reset example above to reproduce this result, then replace the values with your own.

Scope and limitations

Inputs may be whole numbers, negatives, or finite decimals. Decimal values are converted to base-10 fractions before exact integer arithmetic and simplification. The browser reads inputs as JavaScript numbers, so digits beyond its numeric precision cannot be recovered. Denominators must not be zero, and division also requires a nonzero second numerator. Results are rounded to 4 decimal places for display only; the underlying fraction is exact for integer inputs.

FAQ

What happens if a denominator is zero?

The result is not shown. A fraction with a zero denominator is undefined, so the page asks for a nonzero denominator instead of inventing a number.

How is the result simplified?

By the greatest common divisor: for example 4/6 divides by GCD 2 to give 2/3. The sign is kept in the numerator, so −1/2 and 1/−2 both display as −1/2.

Why does the decimal sometimes repeat?

Fractions such as 1/3 have no exact finite decimal. The decimal shown is rounded to 4 places (0.3333); the fraction itself, 1/3, is the exact answer.

Can I use negative fractions?

Yes. Enter a negative numerator (or denominator); the result is normalised so the denominator stays positive.

What is a mixed number?

A whole part plus a fraction, for example 7/3 = 2 1/3. It is shown for improper results; proper fractions and whole numbers are shown as they are.
