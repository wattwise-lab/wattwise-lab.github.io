Formula

Monthly cost = calls × (input tokens × input price + output tokens × output price) ÷ 1,000,000; prices na USD for each million tokens. Cost for each call = monthly cost ÷ calls.

Example wey show the calculation

Input na 2,500 tokens, output na 800 tokens, calls na 100,000, input price na $5 and output price na $20 for each million tokens. Input cost = 100,000 × 2,500 × 5 ÷ 1,000,000 = $1,250. Output cost = 100,000 × 800 × 20 ÷ 1,000,000 = $1,600. Total = $2,850 every month, or $0.0285 for each call. These prices na example only — enter your own plan prices to get figure wey you fit use.

Where e fit work and limits

All prices na examples wey you fit edit for USD per million tokens, no be current provider quote. Zero calls mean zero monthly cost; hypothetical cost for each call still follow your token and price assumptions. Caching, reasoning tokens, discounts, other modalities, taxes and rate limits need separate entries or estimates.

Questions wey people dey ask often

How una calculate am?

Each call cost na input tokens times input price plus output tokens times output price, divide by 1,000,000; that per-call cost times monthly call count na the monthly total.

Na real provider prices be the default prices?

No. Na example inputs be dem. Provider prices dey change and differ by model and tier, so enter the prices from your own plan or invoice before you use the result.

Why output cost dey often pass input cost?

Output prices per token usually higher pass input prices, and long outputs dey accumulate. The breakdown table show the split so you fit see which side you suppose optimise.

Wetin dem no include?

Cached or discounted tokens, batch pricing, retries, infrastructure, and staff time. If those ones matter for you, add dem separately instead make you treat this total as full cost of ownership.

How I fit estimate tokens per call?

Measure sample of real calls: record input and output token counts, find the average, and check again after prompts change. Averages from small short test calls usually understate production calls.

E go predict my bill exactly?

No. Na estimate from your averages and prices. Compare am with actual invoice for one month, then adjust token averages or prices until the model track reality.
