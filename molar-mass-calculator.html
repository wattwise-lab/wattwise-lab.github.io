Formula

Molar mass in g/mol = Σ (number of atoms of each element × its tabulated atomic weight). For Ca(OH)2, the subscript 2 multiplies both O and H: Ca + 2 × O + 2 × H. The fixed table uses rounded values such as H 1.008, C 12.011, N 14.007 and O 15.999.

Worked example

Using this table, H2O has molar mass 2 × 1.008 + 15.999 = 18.015 g/mol. Its calculated mass fractions are 2.016 ÷ 18.015 ≈ 11.19% hydrogen and 15.999 ÷ 18.015 ≈ 88.81% oxygen. Other table-based checks are CO2 = 12.011 + 2 × 15.999 = 44.009 g/mol and NaCl = 22.990 + 35.45 = 58.440 g/mol.

Scope and limitations

This calculator uses a fixed table of 38 common elements with rounded conventional atomic weights. Enter element symbols, positive integer counts and parentheses, using at most 200 characters and 32 nested groups. Charges, isotope labels, structural formulas and dot hydrates are not supported; for CuSO4·5H2O enter CuSO4H10O5. Natural isotopic composition can change atomic weights, so these estimates do not represent a sample-specific laboratory measurement. FAQ

What is a mole in chemistry?

One mole contains exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ specified elementary entities. Molar mass expresses the mass of one mole in g/mol; the displayed values use this calculator’s rounded atomic-weight table.

Why is water 18.015 and not 18?

Hydrogen and oxygen use averaged atomic weights of 1.008 and 15.999 in this table. Therefore 2 × 1.008 + 15.999 = 18.015 g/mol. This is an approximate conventional value; an isotope-enriched sample can differ.

How do parentheses work?

The number after a closing parenthesis multiplies everything inside it. In Ca(OH)2, the 2 doubles both O and H: Ca + O2 + H2, giving 40.078 + 2 × 15.999 + 2 × 1.008 = 74.092 g/mol.

What if my element is not recognised?

The supported symbols are listed above. An unknown symbol produces an error; the calculator does not guess a mass or invent a value for an unsupported element.

How do I use molar mass in practice?

To convert mass to moles, divide grams by molar mass; to convert moles to mass, multiply. For example, 36.03 g of water is 36.03 ÷ 18.015 = 2.00 moles.

Is molar mass the same as molecular weight?

Numerically yes, units differ: molecular weight is a relative number (unitless), molar mass is that number expressed in g/mol. People use the terms interchangeably in most contexts.
