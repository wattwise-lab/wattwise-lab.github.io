Formula

Monthly rate = annual rate ÷ 12. Monthly payment = principal × monthly rate × (1 + monthly rate)payments ÷ [(1 + monthly rate)payments − 1]. Total interest = payment × payments − principal. If rate na 0%, payment na principal ÷ payments.

Example wey show the calculation

Principal na $300,000 at 6.5% for 30 years (360 payments): monthly payment na $1,896.20, total interest na $382,633.47, total paid na $682,633.47. For the first year, $19,401.27 go to interest and $3,353.18 go to principal, leaving $296,646.82.

Where e fit work and limits

Rate remain fixed, no fees, tax or insurance, and you pay on time every month. We no model variable rates, balloon payments, prepayment penalties or payment holidays. Extra payments dey reduce principal for the month wey you pay dem.

Questions wey people dey ask often

Why interest for the first year dey so high?

We dey charge interest on the full balance, so early payments mostly na interest. As balance reduce, the same payment go cover more principal.

Extra payments really dey save that much?

Yes, because every extra dollar dey reduce the balance wey future interest go apply to. The savings dey build for the rest of the loan term.

Wetin go happen if rate na 0%?

Payment na simply principal divided by number of payments, and total interest na $0.

This one na the same as mortgage payment?

Only the principal-and-interest part. Mortgage bill usually add tax, insurance and sometimes PMI and HOA; the mortgage calculator dey cover those ones.

Wetin if I miss one payment?

We no model am here. Missed or late payments usually add fees and interest and push the payoff date further.
