Formula

Energy (kWh) = calls × Wh per call × PUE ÷ 1,000. Emissions (kg CO₂e) = energy × grid grams per kWh ÷ 1,000. Per-call emissions (g CO₂e) = Wh per call × PUE × grid grams per kWh ÷ 1,000.

Worked example

Calls 100,000, energy 0.5 Wh per call, PUE 1.2, grid 450 gCO₂e/kWh. Energy = 100,000 × 0.5 × 1.2 ÷ 1,000 = 60 kWh. Emissions = 60 × 450 ÷ 1,000 = 27 kg CO₂e. Per call: 0.5 × 1.2 × 450 ÷ 1,000 = 0.27 g CO₂e; per 1,000 calls: 270 g CO₂e. These inputs are examples only — replace the energy-per-call and grid figures with your own before relying on the result.

Scope and limitations

This is a scenario model, not a carbon accounting result. It covers only the inference energy you describe: training, model development, hardware manufacturing, networking, cooling beyond PUE, and embodied emissions are excluded. Energy per call varies by model, hardware, batching, and prompt length, and grid intensity varies by place and time — both are user inputs here precisely because no single published value fits every case. No live emissions data or certified accounting is provided.

FAQ

How is this calculated?

Energy is calls times Wh per call times PUE, divided by 1,000 to get kWh; emissions are energy times grid grams per kWh, divided by 1,000 to get kilograms of CO₂e.

Why are the carbon factor and energy per call editable?

Because they are assumptions, not constants. A different model, GPU, or region gives different values, so the page treats them as inputs you must supply and labels the defaults as examples only.

What does PUE mean here?

Power usage effectiveness: total facility energy divided by IT energy. A PUE of 1.2 means 20% overhead for cooling and other facility loads on top of inference energy; 1.0 means no overhead is counted.

Does this include training the model?

No. It covers only the inference calls in your scenario. Training, fine-tuning, and hardware manufacturing are separate and much larger in some cases, and are not modeled here.

Why is the result linear in call count?

Each row in the volume table reuses the same per-call figure, so doubling calls doubles the estimate. Real systems may batch or cache, which would break that linearity — another reason to replace the example per-call figure with measured data.

Can I use this for an official emissions report?

No. It is a planning estimate from your own assumptions. Formal reporting needs measured energy data, a documented grid factor for the right region and period, and the reporting framework your organisation follows.
