Formula

Monthly shortfall = monthly expenses − monthly emergency income. Usable cash = cash × (1 − buffer%). Runway = usable cash ÷ monthly shortfall, when the shortfall is positive. If income covers expenses, the runway is not limited by cash.

Worked example

Cash $120,000 with a 10% buffer: usable cash is $120,000 × 90% = $108,000, kept back $12,000. Monthly shortfall is $15,000 − $5,000 = $10,000, so runway is $108,000 ÷ $10,000 = 10.8 months.

Scope and limitations

Expenses and emergency income are assumed flat every month, with no interest earned on cash, no new debts, no one-off costs, and no change in the buffer. Real emergencies are lumpy: a single large bill shortens the runway more than the smooth monthly average suggests.

FAQ

What is the buffer for?

Money you deliberately do not spend: a floor for non-negotiable costs, a deposit, or a reserve for the month after the emergency. A larger buffer shortens the runway but leaves a cushion.

What if my emergency income covers my expenses?

Then the shortfall is zero or negative and cash is not being drained: the runway is not limited by cash on these figures. The calculator says so rather than dividing by zero.

Should expenses be my normal spending or bare-minimum spending?

Bare-minimum essential spending gives the true emergency runway. Normal spending gives a shorter, more conservative figure; run both and compare.

Does cash earn interest while I draw on it?

Not here. Interest on a cash pile is small relative to monthly spending, and assuming none keeps the estimate conservative.

How much runway is enough?

That depends on your situation and is not a number this calculator prescribes. Common planning horizons are a few months to a year; the sensitivity table above shows what each buffer level costs in months.
