Formula

Mean = Σx ÷ n. Population SD = √[Σ(x − mean)² ÷ n]; sample SD uses n − 1 in place of n. Population variance is Σ(x − mean)² ÷ n and sample variance is Σ(x − mean)² ÷ (n − 1).

Worked example

For 12, 15, 18, 20, 25: mean = 90 ÷ 5 = 18. Squared deviations are 36, 9, 0, 4 and 49, summing to 98. Population SD = √(98 ÷ 5) = √19.6 = 4.4272 (4.427189 to more places). Sample SD = √(98 ÷ 4) = √24.5 = 4.9497. Use Reset example above to reproduce this result, then replace the list with your own data.

Scope and limitations

Every list entry must be a finite number. Use population SD when the list is the complete group of interest, and sample SD when observations estimate a larger population. Sample SD is undefined for a single value. Standard deviation is sensitive to outliers and describes spread, not distribution shape. Display is rounded to 4 decimal places.

FAQ

Population or sample SD — which do I use?

Population SD (÷ n) when your numbers are the whole group you care about. Sample SD (÷ n − 1) when they are a sample and you are estimating the spread of a larger population.

Why is sample SD larger?

It divides by n − 1 instead of n, which corrects for the fact that a sample mean is fitted to the sample itself. The gap shrinks as n grows.

What if I enter only one number?

Population SD is 0 (no spread), and sample SD is undefined, shown as —, because n − 1 is zero.

Does the order of the numbers matter?

No. Mean and standard deviation are unchanged by reordering the list.

Are non-numeric entries an error?

Yes. Invalid or non-finite entries pause calculation and show an error instead of being silently skipped. Correct the list before reading a result.
