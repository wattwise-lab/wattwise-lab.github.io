Formula

DTI = total monthly debt payments ÷ gross monthly income × 100. Housing ratio = housing payment ÷ gross monthly income × 100. Income here is gross (before tax), because that is the figure lenders use.

Worked example

Income $6,000 a month, housing $1,500, auto $300, other debts $200: total debts are $2,000, so DTI = $2,000 ÷ $6,000 = 33.33%. The housing ratio alone is $1,500 ÷ $6,000 = 25.00%. Both sit in the range lenders commonly describe as manageable (at or under 36% for total DTI).

Scope and limitations

The bands below are common lender guidelines, not rules: individual lenders weigh credit history, savings, and loan type alongside DTI, and some loan programs allow higher ratios. Only required minimum payments count — paying extra voluntarily does not raise your DTI, and everyday spending (food, utilities, subscriptions) is not debt and is not included. Self-employed income is harder to document; lenders often use an average of recent tax years rather than one good month.

FAQ

What counts as a debt payment?

Required minimum payments: mortgage or rent, auto loans, student loans, personal loans, and credit card minimums. Child support and alimony count where they apply. Groceries, utilities, and subscriptions do not.

What is the difference between front-end and back-end DTI?

Front-end is housing only (25.00% in the example); back-end is all debts (33.33%). Lenders usually care most about back-end, but some also cap front-end, often around 28%.

Is 36% a hard limit?

No. Many lenders prefer 36% or under, some accept up to 43%, and certain programs go higher with compensating factors. Treat the bands as guidance, not approval odds.

Should I use gross or take-home pay?

Gross, before tax. Using take-home pay would overstate the ratio and mismatch how lenders compute it.

How do I lower my DTI?

Pay down a small debt entirely (removing its payment helps more than trimming a large balance), avoid new monthly obligations before applying, and report all stable income — a side income with a paper trail counts.

Does paying extra on a loan help my DTI?

Not until the loan is gone. DTI uses the required minimum payment, so extra payments only help once the debt is fully paid off and its payment disappears.
