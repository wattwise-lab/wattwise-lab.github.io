Formula

Monthly cost = office days × (daily transport + extra food + commute minutes ÷ 60 × hourly value). Cost per office day = daily transport + extra food + commute minutes ÷ 60 × hourly value.

Worked example

Office days 12, transport $55, extra food $35, commute 100 minutes, hourly value $80. Per day: $55 + $35 + 100 ÷ 60 × $80 = $90 + $133.33 = $223.33. Monthly = 12 × $223.33 = $2,680. Cash half = 12 × $90 = $1,080; time half = 12 × $133.33 = $1,600; commute time = 20 hours per month.

Scope and limitations

The result combines cash costs and time value: it is not a pure cash outlay, and the cash half alone is $1,080 in the worked example. Enter costs after any reimbursement, or the cash half is overstated. Career benefits, collaboration, office conditions, and wellbeing effects in either direction are outside this model, as are clothing, vehicle wear beyond the transport input, and parking unless included in it.

FAQ

How is this calculated?

Each office day costs daily transport plus extra food plus commute minutes divided by 60 times hourly value; multiply by office days per month.

Is the total all cash I spend?

No. Only the cash half (transport plus food) leaves your wallet. The time half values commute hours at your hourly rate — set hourly value to zero for a cash-only figure.

What counts as extra food?

Food spending above what you would spend working from home: bought lunches, coffee, and snacks, net of home food you no longer eat. Do not count your full lunch bill if you would eat lunch anyway.

Should transport be after reimbursement?

Yes. Enter what you actually pay after any employer travel subsidy or pass discount, or the cost is overstated.

What if my commute is productive?

Then valuing every minute at full hourly rate overstates the cost. Reduce the hourly value for commute time, or count only the unproductive share of the commute in minutes.

Does this say office work is not worth it?

No. It prices one side of the decision. Collaboration, mentoring, visibility, and personal preference are real benefits that this money-and-time model does not price.
