Formula

Net volume = length × width × depth. Waste volume = net volume × waste rate. Order volume = net volume × (1 + waste rate).

Worked example

Length 10 m, width 4 m, depth 0.15 m: net volume = 10 × 4 × 0.15 = 6 m³. At an 8% waste allowance, waste volume = 6.00 × 0.08 = 0.48 m³, so order volume = 6.48 m³ (228.84 ft³). These are the example defaults above; replace them with your own measurements.

Scope and limitations

This covers a single rectangular pour only. Subgrade variation, reinforcement displacement, irregular shapes, sloped sections, supplier minimum loads and rounding to the supplier's ordering unit can all change the order. No price is calculated: enter no price here, so compare supplier quotes against the order volume yourself.

FAQ

Why add a waste allowance at all?

Because a real pour rarely matches the exact rectangular volume: spillage, uneven subgrade and formwork gaps all consume extra concrete. The allowance is your buffer, not a supplier charge.

What if my shape is not a rectangle?

Split it into measured rectangles, calculate each one, and add the net volumes before applying waste. Do not force an irregular shape into one rectangle and hope the waste allowance absorbs the difference.

How do I measure depth?

Measure the finished slab thickness at several points and use a representative value. If depth varies a lot, split the pour into zones with different depths instead of averaging blindly.

Does this include the supplier's minimum load?

No. If your order is below a supplier minimum, the supplier may still charge for the minimum. Check that before ordering, especially for small pours.

Can I convert the result to cubic feet or litres?

Yes: 1 m³ = 35.3147 ft³ = 1,000 litres. The breakdown table shows the order in all three units.

Does this tell me what the concrete will cost?

No, there is no price input on this page. Multiply the order volume by your supplier's quoted price per unit yourself; prices vary by mix, location and delivery terms.
