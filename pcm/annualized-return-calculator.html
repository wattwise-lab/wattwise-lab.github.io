Formula

Yearly compounded return = (final value ÷ initial value)1 ÷ years − 1. Total return = (final value − initial value) ÷ initial value.

Example wey show the calculation

If $10,000 wey you start with grow reach $15,000 inside 4 years: total gain na $5,000, total return na 50.00%, and yearly compounded return na (15,000 ÷ 10,000)1/4 − 1 = 10.67%. Na the steady yearly rate wey fit carry $10,000 reach $15,000 inside 4 years.

Where e fit work and limits

This smooth rate look back at wetin don happen. E assume say growth compound evenly, and e no explain price swings, fees, tax, inflation or money wey you add or remove during that time. You no fit compare am directly with simple average yearly return, and e no be forecast.

Questions wey people dey ask often

Na the same thing annualized return and average yearly return be?

No. Simple average of yearly returns dey ignore compounding. Annualized (compound) rate na the single steady rate wey dey reproduce total growth over the period.

Wetin if final value lower pass initial value?

Result negative, and e correct: negative annualized rate mean say value shrink at that steady yearly pace.

I fit use am for periods wey short pass one year?

Yes, enter years as fraction, like 0.5 for six months. But when you turn short period into yearly rate, short-term changes go look bigger, so use the result with care.

E dey include dividends or fees?

Only if dem already reflect for final value wey you enter. Reinvested dividends dey raise final value; fees paid out dey lower am.

Why e lower pass total return divide by years?

Because compounding dey do part of the work: 50% over 4 years na 10.67% compounded, not 12.50% a year, since each year growth also earn growth in later years.
