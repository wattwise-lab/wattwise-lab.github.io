Formula

DTI = total monthly payments wey you enter ÷ gross monthly income × 100. Housing share = housing payment ÷ gross monthly income × 100. Gross income mean income before taxes and deductions.

Example wey show the calculation

Gross income of $6,000 every month, housing of $1,500, vehicle payment of $300 and other debts of $200 give total monthly payments of $2,000. DTI = $2,000 ÷ $6,000 × 100 = 33.33%. Housing share = $1,500 ÷ $6,000 × 100 = 25.00%. These na ratios, no be predictions of loan approval.

Where e fit work and limits

Dis worksheet total di payments wey you enter. Different lenders and loan products use different DTI limits and fit define eligible income and housing obligations differently. E no assess credit history, assets, local lending rules or whether you fit afford all living costs. Confirm payments and income wey your lender include; avoid counting di same payment twice.

Questions wey people dey ask often

Which payments I suppose enter?

Use recurring obligations wey relevant to your comparison, like housing, vehicle loans, student loans and credit card minimums. Dis worksheet include any housing payment wey you enter, including rent. Lender fit use different definitions. Food and utilities na separate living costs; di ratio no replace full budget.

Dis ratio dey determine loan approval?

No. No universal DTI cutoff dey. Di lender, product, income documents and other eligibility factors affect di decision. Dis calculator show di ratio without assigning approval category.

Make I use income before tax abi take-home pay?

Use gross income before taxes and deductions for dis DTI calculation. Separate budget based on take-home pay dey useful for everyday affordability, but e answer different question.

How payment changes dey affect di ratio?

If income no change, reducing required monthly payment go lower di ratio. Paying extra principal change dis calculation only when e change di recurring payment wey you enter. Ask your lender how dem assess revised payments and documented income.
