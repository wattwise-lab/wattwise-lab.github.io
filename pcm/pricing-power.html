Formula

New price = price × (1 + increase%). New volume = sales × (1 − volume decline%). New profit = (new price − cost) × new volume. Current profit = (price − cost) × sales. Profit change = new profit − current profit.

Example wey show the calculation

Price $199, cost $110, sales 1,000 units, increase 8%, volume decline 5%: new price = $199 × 1.08 = $214.92, new volume = 1,000 × 0.95 = 950 units, new profit = ($214.92 − $110) × 950 = $99,674, current profit = ($199 − $110) × 1,000 = $89,000, so profit dey rise by $10,674.

Where e fit work and limits

Unit cost stay constant and na you dey enter the volume decline; we no dey predict am. We no model fixed costs, competitors' response, customers wey fit switch with time or demand shifts — use the result to compare scenarios, no treat am as forecast.

Questions wey people dey ask often

How una calculate am?

Profit change = (price × (1 + increase%) − cost) × sales × (1 − volume decline%) − (price − cost) × sales. Multiply new margin per unit by new volume, then subtract today's profit.

Wetin the sales volume drop input mean?

Enter the sales-volume percentage wey you expect to lose after price increase. The formula dey apply that percentage directly to current sales. E no estimate demand or multiply price-elasticity coefficient by price change.

Price increase fit make me lose money?

Yes. If sales volume wey you lose outweigh the extra margin per unit, new profit go fall below current profit. Na that trade-off this calculator dey show.

Why cost for each unit remain the same?

Volume changes fit change unit cost for real life, but this model dey separate the price-volume trade-off. If your unit cost dey change with volume, adjust the cost input and compare scenarios.

What about fixed costs?

Dem dey cancel for the change: both scenarios get the same fixed costs, so na unit margin and volume alone dey drive the profit difference.

I suppose use only this result decide my price?

No. Use customer research, competitors' prices and margin check together with am. The volume decline wey you enter na the input wey uncertainty pass for, so test several values.
