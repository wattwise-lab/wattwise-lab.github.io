Formula

Mean = sum of all values ÷ count. Median = the middle value of the sorted data (or the average of the two middle values when the count is even). Mode = the value that appears most often; a data set can have several modes, or none when every value appears once.

Worked example

Data 4, 8, 6, 5, 3, 8, 9: sorted, that is 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 8, 9. Sum = 43, count = 7, so mean = 43 ÷ 7 = 6.14. The middle (4th) value is 6, so the median is 6. The value 8 appears twice — more than any other — so the mode is 8. Range = 9 − 3 = 6.

Scope and limitations

These are descriptive measures of the data you enter — they describe this set, not a wider population, and they say nothing about spread on their own (pair them with range or standard deviation for that). The mean is pulled by outliers: one very large value raises it even when most values are small, which is exactly when the median is the better summary. Modes are only meaningful for data where repeats are expected; with continuous measurements, exact repeats are rare and the mode may not exist.

FAQ

Mean or median — which should I report?

Report the mean for roughly symmetric data and the median when the data is skewed or has outliers. Income is the classic case: a few very high incomes raise the mean well above what a typical person earns, so median income is the standard headline figure.

What if there are two middle values?

With an even count, the median is the average of the two middle values. For 2, 4, 7, 9 the median is (4 + 7) ÷ 2 = 5.50.

Can there be more than one mode?

Yes. If two values tie for the highest frequency, the data is bimodal and both are modes. This page lists all tied values.

What does "no mode" mean?

Every value appears exactly once, so nothing is most frequent. That is common with continuous measurements like heights recorded to the millimetre.

Do I need to sort the data first?

No. Sorting is done for you; the median and the sorted list are computed from whatever order you paste in.

Are commas required?

No. Commas, spaces, and line breaks all work as separators, so pasted spreadsheet columns work directly.
