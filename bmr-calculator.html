Bars are scaled to the larger of the two values. The only difference between the two bars is the sex constant (+5 for male, −161 for female) in the Mifflin–St Jeor equation.

Formula

BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age (years) + 5 for men or − 161 for women. This is the Mifflin–St Jeor equation.

Worked example

Weight 65 kg, height 170 cm, age 30, female: BMR = 10 × 65 + 6.25 × 170 − 5 × 30 − 161 = 650 + 1,062.5 − 150 − 161 = 1,401.5 kcal/day. That is 9,811 kcal per week and 42,045 kcal per 30 days. With the male constant (+5 instead of −161) the same inputs give 1,567.5 kcal/day. Use Reset example above to reproduce this result.

Scope and limitations

BMR is resting expenditure only — it excludes activity, digestion, and exercise, so it is lower than total daily expenditure. The Mifflin–St Jeor equation is a population estimate: it was fitted to group averages, so individual results vary with body composition, illness, medications, and other factors the inputs do not capture. It is not a metabolic test and not a personal energy prescription.

This page is for adult educational estimates. The original Mifflin–St Jeor study used healthy adults aged 19–78; extrapolation and individual medical circumstances require care. This is not a measured metabolic rate or an instruction for calorie intake.

FAQ

What is BMR?

The energy the body uses at complete rest to maintain basic functions. Any activity on top of rest adds to it, which is why total daily expenditure is higher.

Why do men and women get different results?

The Mifflin–St Jeor equation adds +5 for men and subtracts 161 for women, reflecting average differences in the populations the equation was fitted to. It is a group adjustment, not a measurement of any individual.

Is BMR the same as the calories I need each day?

No. Daily need also includes activity. Multiply BMR by an activity factor — or use the TDEE Calculator — for a total daily estimate.

How accurate is this?

It is an estimate from a population equation. Individual values can differ materially; only a metabolic test measures resting expenditure directly.

Is my data stored?

No. This calculation runs locally in your browser and the values you enter are not sent to a CalcMintly database.

Does age matter much?

Yes, through the −5 × age term: each additional year lowers the estimate by 5 kcal/day at fixed weight and height.
