METHODOLOGY

Transparent inputs, visible formulas, dated sources.

Our tools are designed to help a homeowner frame a decision—not to replace a utility tariff, installer quote, licensed design or tax advice.

Data hierarchy

We prefer U.S. government datasets and national laboratories, followed by manufacturer specifications supplied by the user. Every default must be editable because public averages are not personal facts.

Electricity prices

The homepage uses EIA's 2024 State Electricity Profiles. Those figures are average retail prices across end-use sectors, not a guaranteed residential tariff. The calculator therefore lets the user replace the default with the cents-per-kWh value from a current bill.

Solar model

The simplified solar calculator derives consumption from bill and rate, then sizes production from peak-sun hours and system losses. NREL PVWatts remains the preferred next step for location-specific solar resource, roof orientation and more detailed loss modeling.

Battery model

Deliverable energy equals nameplate capacity after reserve and inverter efficiency. Runtime equals deliverable watt-hours divided by average watts. Startup surge, equipment limits and electrical-code requirements remain outside scope.

EV model

EV energy equals miles multiplied by kWh per 100 miles, adjusted for charging loss. Gasoline energy cost equals miles divided by MPG, multiplied by dollars per gallon.

Corrections

We date methodology reviews and correct formulas or source descriptions when new evidence warrants it. Contact us with the page, input values and expected result so the calculation can be reproduced.
