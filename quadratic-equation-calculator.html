Formula

x = [−b ± √(b² − 4ac)] ÷ 2a. The discriminant D = b² − 4ac decides the root type: D > 0 gives two distinct real roots, D = 0 gives one repeated root, D < 0 gives two complex roots. The vertex (turning point) sits at x = −b ÷ 2a.

Worked example

x² − 5x + 6 = 0: a = 1, b = −5, c = 6. Discriminant = (−5)² − 4 × 1 × 6 = 25 − 24 = 1. Roots = [5 ± √1] ÷ 2, so x = 3 and x = 2. Check: 3² − 15 + 6 = 0 and 2² − 10 + 6 = 0. The vertex is at x = 2.5.

Scope and limitations

Nonzero a defines a quadratic. If a is zero, the page reports the linear or constant-equation case. Results normally show 12 significant digits, increasing to 17 to distinguish close roots. Very small or large values use scientific notation. Decimal input precision still limits accuracy, especially near repeated roots. Unsupported numerical magnitudes show an error instead of infinity or a false zero.

W. Kahan (2004), Qdrtcs

FAQ

What if a is zero?

Then solve bx + c = 0. If b is nonzero, x = −c ÷ b. If b and c are both zero, every real x works; if b is zero but c is not, there is no solution.

What does the discriminant tell me?

Whether the roots are real and how many: positive means two distinct real roots, zero means one repeated root, negative means no real roots — the parabola never crosses the x-axis.

What are complex roots?

When the discriminant is negative, the roots involve i (the square root of −1). For x² + 2x + 5 = 0 the roots are −1 + 2i and −1 − 2i. They are genuine solutions over the complex numbers, just not points on the real x-axis.

Why two roots?

A polynomial of degree 2 has two roots counting multiplicity. They can coincide (one repeated root) or be a complex pair, but there are always two.

Should I factor instead?

Factoring is faster when the roots are small integers, as in the example (x − 3)(x − 2). The formula always works, including when factoring is not obvious.

Does the vertex matter?

It is the parabola's turning point: the minimum when a > 0, the maximum when a < 0. It sits exactly halfway between the two real roots when they exist.
