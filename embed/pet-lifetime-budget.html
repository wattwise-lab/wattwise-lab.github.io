Formula

For n remaining years and growth r: budget = annual cost × ((1 + r)n − 1) ÷ r + reserve. With zero growth, use annual cost × n + reserve. Remaining years n = expected lifespan − current age.

Worked example

Age 4, expected lifespan 14 (10 remaining years), annual cost 9,000, growth 4%, reserve 15,000: growth-adjusted costs = 9,000 × ((1.0410 − 1) ÷ 0.04) = 108,054.96, plus the 15,000 reserve = 123,054.96. All figures here are example inputs, not veterinary or market data — replace them with your own costs.

Scope and limitations

This is a nominal budget using an assumed lifespan and constant cost growth, not a veterinary or insurance estimate. It excludes nothing by name, but it only contains what you put in: if insurance, food, routine care and emergencies are not inside your annual cost or reserve, they are not in the result either. Lifespan is uncertain; test shorter and longer lifespans. Remaining years cannot be negative.

FAQ

How is this calculated?

For n remaining years and growth r: budget = annual cost × ((1+r)n − 1) ÷ r + reserve. With zero growth, use annual cost × n + reserve.

What does the reserve cover?

Whatever you intend it to cover — typically emergencies or end-of-life care. It is a lump sum added once, not an annual amount.

What does this estimate exclude?

Anything not inside your annual cost or reserve. It is not a veterinary quote and not an insurance estimate; it is a budgeting model built from your assumptions.

Why does cost growth matter so much?

Because it compounds: at 4% over 10 years the growth-adjusted total (108,054.96) is well above 10 flat years at 9,000 (90,000). Small rate changes compound into large differences.

What if my pet lives longer than expected?

The budget rises with every extra year. Run the calculation at a longer lifespan as a sensitivity check, not just the central estimate.

Does CalcMintly store my inputs?

No. This calculation runs locally in your browser and the values you enter are not sent to a CalcMintly database.
