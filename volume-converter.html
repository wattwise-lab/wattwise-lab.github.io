All four units describe the same volume; gallons here are US liquid gallons, not UK imperial gallons.

Formula

Convert the source value to litres, then divide by the target unit's litre factor: target = value × source factor ÷ target factor.

UnitLitres per unit

US liquid gallon (gal)3.785411784

The US gallon factor is exact by definition. A UK imperial gallon is larger (4.54609 L) and is not used here.

Worked example

1 L ÷ 3.785411784 L/gal = 0.264172 US gal, and 1 L = 1,000 mL = 0.001 m³. Conversely, 10 US gal × 3.785411784 = 37.854118 L.

Scope and limitations

Negative volumes are rejected as invalid. Display values are rounded to 6 decimal places; the underlying calculation is not rounded. If your recipe or tank uses imperial gallons, multiply the US-gallon result by 0.832674 to get imperial gallons (since 3.785411784 ÷ 4.54609 = 0.832674).

FAQ

Is this a US or UK gallon?

US liquid gallon (3.785411784 L). A UK imperial gallon is 4.54609 L, about 20% larger — mixing them up is the most common volume-conversion error.

How many litres in a cubic metre?

1,000 litres, exactly.

How many millilitres in a litre?

1,000 mL, exactly.

What about fluid ounces?

Not on this page: 1 US fluid ounce = 0.0295735295625 L. Convert fluid ounces to litres first and enter that value.

Does temperature change the result?

No — this is a pure unit conversion. Real liquids expand with temperature, but that is a physical measurement question, not a unit conversion.
