Formula

Area = π × radius². Circumference = 2 × π × radius. Diameter = 2 × radius. π is used at full precision and results are rounded to two decimals for display.

Worked example

Radius 5: area = π × 5² = π × 25 = 78.54. Circumference = 2 × π × 5 = 31.42. Diameter = 10. If the radius was 5 cm, the area is 78.54 cm² and the circumference is 31.42 cm.

Scope and limitations

This is a perfect circle: one radius, one centre. Ellipses, arcs, sectors, and rings need their own formulas and are not covered here. The page does not convert units — a radius in inches gives an area in square inches; convert first if you need another unit.

FAQ

Radius or diameter — which do I enter?

Radius: the distance from the centre to the edge. If you only know the diameter (edge to edge through the centre), halve it first — or read the diameter chip above in reverse.

Why is area in square units?

Because area covers a surface. A radius of 5 cm gives 78.54 cm², not 78.54 cm. Mixing the two is the most common circle mistake.

What value of pi is used?

Full double-precision pi (3.141592653589793…), not 3.14. The difference only matters past the second decimal, but there is no reason to round early.

How does area grow with radius?

With the square: doubling the radius quadruples the area. A radius-10 circle has area 314.16, exactly four times the radius-5 area of 78.54.

Can I use this for a sphere?

No. A sphere's surface area is 4πr² and its volume is (4/3)πr³ — different formulas for a 3D object.

What if I know the circumference instead?

Divide it by 2π to get the radius, then enter that here. For a circumference of 31.42, the radius is 31.42 ÷ 6.2832 = 5.
