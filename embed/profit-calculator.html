Formula

Net profit = revenue − variable costs − fixed costs. Net margin = net profit ÷ revenue × 100%. Total cost = variable costs + fixed costs. Cost ratio = total cost ÷ revenue × 100%.

Worked example

Revenue $100,000, variable costs $68,000, fixed costs $8,000: total cost = $76,000, net profit = $100,000 − $76,000 = $24,000, net margin = $24,000 ÷ $100,000 × 100% = 24.00%, and cost ratio = 76.00%.

Scope and limitations

This is an operating view: interest, taxes, depreciation, and one-off items are not separated out. If your cost figures already include them, the margin reflects that; if not, it does not. Costs are treated as fixed totals for the period, not per-unit rates.

FAQ

How does the profit calculator work?

Net profit = revenue − variable costs − fixed costs; net margin = net profit ÷ revenue. It splits a period's revenue into what costs consumed and what remained.

What is the difference between variable and fixed costs?

Variable costs move with output — materials, per-order fees. Fixed costs do not — rent, base salaries. The split matters for break-even analysis; for net profit only the total matters.

What does a negative net profit mean?

Costs exceeded revenue for the period: a loss of that amount. The margin is negative by the same proportion.

Is net margin the same as gross margin?

No. Gross margin stops at cost of goods sold; net margin counts all variable and fixed costs entered here, so it is usually lower.

Does this include tax?

No, unless tax is already inside the cost figures you enter. Apply tax treatment separately to the net profit.

Why does margin show a dash at zero revenue?

Margin divides by revenue, so at zero revenue it is undefined. Profit itself is still shown as negative total costs.
