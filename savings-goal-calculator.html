What makes up the target

Bars are scaled to the target. Interest on the contributions is the part of the target neither your current savings nor your monthly payments cover directly.

Balance by year on this plan

YearBalance

Contributions are end-of-month and the rate is fixed. The final year lands on the target when the gap is positive.

Formula

Monthly rate = annual rate ÷ 12. Grown current amount = current amount × (1 + monthly rate)months. Required contribution = (target − grown current amount) ÷ [((1 + monthly rate)months − 1) ÷ monthly rate]. If the grown current amount already reaches the target, the required contribution is $0.

Worked example

Target $50,000 in 5 years, starting from $5,000 at 3%: the $5,000 grows to $5,808.08, leaving a gap of $44,191.92. Spread over 60 end-of-month contributions at 0.25% a month, the required contribution is $44,191.92 ÷ 64.65 = $683.59 a month.

Scope and limitations

The rate is assumed constant, contributions are never missed, and taxes, fees, and inflation are ignored, so the target is in nominal dollars. If the current amount already grows past the target, no contribution is required and the plan is already funded on these assumptions.

FAQ

What if my current savings already grow past the target?

Then the required contribution is $0. The calculator shows the grown current amount above the target, and you do not need to add anything on these assumptions.

Why does a higher rate lower the monthly amount?

Because interest covers more of the gap. But a higher assumed rate is also a riskier assumption: if the real return is lower, the plan falls short.

Are contributions at the start or end of the month?

End of the month, matching the source calculator. Starting a month earlier would lower the required amount slightly.

Should the target be adjusted for inflation?

This calculator works in nominal dollars. If the target is a future price, enter the inflated future figure, not today's price.

What if I cannot afford the required amount?

Extend the time horizon, raise the assumed rate only if you can justify it, or lower the target. The calculator shows the trade directly when you change years.
