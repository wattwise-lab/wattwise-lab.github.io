What makes up the total

Bars are scaled to the total. The extra-water input is included in the total bar but has no separate bar, since it is a free-form adjustment.

Formula

Base water = weight (kg) × 35 ml. Add 350 ml per 30 exercise minutes (proportionally for other durations), plus the extra water entered. Total = base + exercise addition + extra.

Worked example

Weight 65 kg, exercise 30 minutes, extra 0 ml: base = 65 × 35 = 2,275 ml; exercise addition = 350 ml; total = 2,625 ml/day (2.63 litres, about 11.1 cups of 8 oz). Use Reset example above to reproduce this result.

Scope and limitations

This is a simple rule-of-thumb formula, not a clinical fluid assessment. It does not account for diet (food provides water too), climate, illness, medical conditions, or medications, all of which can change fluid needs in either direction. The extra-water input is a free-form adjustment you control — it is not derived from any condition you enter. The result is a planning figure, not a prescription for how much to drink.

FAQ

Where does 35 ml per kg come from?

It is the rule-of-thumb coefficient this calculator uses for the base figure. It is a planning convention, not a measured requirement for any individual.

Does food count toward water?

Yes, in reality — food provides a substantial share of daily water. This formula does not split food and drink, so the total is best read as total water from all sources, not a drinking target alone.

What is the extra-water input for?

Hot weather, high altitude, or any other need you want to add on top of the base and exercise figures. You set it yourself; the page does not derive it.

Can drinking this amount be wrong for me?

Possibly. Medical conditions and medications can require more or less fluid, which is why the result is labelled an estimate and clinical advice takes precedence.

Is my data stored?

No. This calculation runs locally in your browser and the values you enter are not sent to a CalcMintly database.

Why is exercise 350 ml per 30 minutes?

That is the fixed exercise coefficient used here, applied proportionally: 15 minutes adds 175 ml, 60 minutes adds 700 ml.
