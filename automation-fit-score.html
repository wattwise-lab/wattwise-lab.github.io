Formula

Score = clamp(4 × rule clarity + 3 × digitisation + min(30, frequency × minutes ÷ 600 × 3) − 2 × risk, 0, 100). All three ratings are on a 0–10 scale and are clamped to that range before scoring.

Worked example

Frequency 80 runs per month, 15 minutes per run, rule clarity 8, digitisation 7, risk 4. Rule points = 4 × 8 = 32. Digitisation points = 3 × 7 = 21. Volume = 80 × 15 ÷ 600 × 3 = 6, below the 30 cap. Risk deduction = 2 × 4 = 8. Total = 32 + 21 + 6 − 8 = 51 out of 100 — a mixed case in the 40–69 review band. Monthly time on the task is 80 × 15 = 1,200 minutes (20 hours). Note: the source page labelled this result in minutes; the unit is points out of 100, and this page corrects that label.

Scope and limitations

This is a custom weighted checklist, not a validated scientific score and not a prediction of automation success. The weights (4, 3, volume cap 30, risk 2) and the 70/40 bands are heuristic choices made for this page; changing them changes the score. It does not assess implementation cost, exception rates, data quality, change management, or regulatory constraints — a high score still needs a pilot, and a low score may still be worth automating for compliance or coverage reasons.

FAQ

How is the score calculated?

Rule clarity times 4, plus digitisation times 3, plus volume points (frequency times minutes divided by 600, times 3, capped at 30), minus risk times 2, with the total clamped between 0 and 100. The full rule table is in the Formula section.

What do the bands mean?

70–100 is read as a strong candidate, 40–69 as review first, below 40 as weak fit. These are this page's own bands for reading the score, not externally validated thresholds.

Why is volume capped at 30?

So sheer volume cannot outweigh unclear rules or undigitised inputs. A task run constantly but governed by judgement calls should not outscore a clear, digitised, lower-volume task by volume alone.

Why does risk subtract only up to 20 points?

Because risk is one factor among four, not a veto. A high-risk task can still score in the review band if everything else is strong — but that is exactly when a human-in-the-loop design, rather than full automation, should be considered.

How should I rate rule clarity and digitisation honestly?

Rule clarity 10 means the decision steps are written down and two people would reach the same outcome; digitisation 10 means inputs and outputs are already structured data. If either needs judgement or paper handling, rate it lower and note why.

Does a high score guarantee savings?

No. It says the task looks automatable, not that the project will pay off. Cost, exception handling, and maintenance still need their own estimate before committing.
