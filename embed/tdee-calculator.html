Formula

TDEE = Mifflin–St Jeor BMR × activity factor, where BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5 for men or − 161 for women. Activity factors used here: 1.2 sedentary, 1.375 lightly active, 1.55 moderately active, 1.725 very active, 1.9 extremely active.

Worked example

Weight 65 kg, height 170 cm, age 30, female, lightly active (1.375): BMR = 10 × 65 + 6.25 × 170 − 5 × 30 − 161 = 1,401.5 kcal/day. TDEE = 1,401.5 × 1.375 = 1,927.1 kcal/day, or 13,490 kcal per week, of which 525.6 kcal/day is the activity increment above BMR. At the sedentary factor the same BMR gives 1,681.8 kcal/day; at extremely active it gives 2,662.9 kcal/day. Use Reset example above to reproduce this result.

Scope and limitations

TDEE inherits the limits of both parts: BMR is a population equation that varies between individuals, and the activity factor is a coarse category chosen by judgment, not a measurement of actual days. Two people picking the same label can have quite different true activity, and day-to-day variation is averaged away. The result is a planning estimate, not a measured daily burn.

This page is for adult educational estimates. The original Mifflin–St Jeor study used healthy adults aged 19–78; extrapolation and individual medical circumstances require care. This is not a measured metabolic rate or an instruction for calorie intake.

FAQ

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR is resting expenditure only. TDEE multiplies BMR by an activity factor to include everything else a day involves.

How do I pick an activity level?

Judge a typical week honestly: mostly sitting is sedentary, light daily movement is lightly active, regular training is moderately active, hard daily training is very active. When in doubt, the lower category is the more cautious estimate.

Why does the category change the result so much?

Because the factor multiplies the whole BMR: moving from 1.2 to 1.9 raises the example result by about 58%, so a wrong category is the largest error source on this page.

Is this a calorie target?

No. It is an estimate of expenditure, not a recommendation for intake. Intake decisions involve goals and circumstances this page does not model.

Is my data stored?

No. This calculation runs locally in your browser and the values you enter are not sent to a CalcMintly database.

Can TDEE change over time?

Yes — weight, age, and activity all feed the equation, so re-entering current values gives a current estimate.
