Formula

Selling price = cost × (1 + markup ÷ 100). Profit per unit = selling price − cost. Margin = profit ÷ selling price × 100. Check the denominator: na selling price we dey divide by, no be cost. That na why margin and markup differ for the same product.

Example wey show the calculation

Cost na $40 with 50% markup: selling price = $40 × 1.50 = $60.00. Profit per unit = $60.00 − $40 = $20.00. Equivalent margin = $20.00 ÷ $60.00 = 33.33%. So the same product get "50% markup" and "33.33% margin" at once; both correct, but dem dey use different bases.

Where e fit work and limits

Cost here na cost for each unit: materials, production or purchase price. Overheads, shipping, payment fees and tax no dey inside unless you add dem to the cost input. The page dey price one unit; multiply by quantity for a batch. Markup below 0% mean say you sell below cost. This page require zero or above, because selling below cost na deliberate loss-leader choice, no be markup calculation.

Questions wey people dey ask often

Markup or margin: which one I suppose use?

Use markup to set price from cost, as shops usually dey do. Use margin to report profit as share of revenue. To change markup to margin: margin = markup ÷ (100 + markup) × 100.

50% markup na the same as 50% margin?

No. 50% markup on $40 go give price of $60 and margin of 33.33%. To get 50% margin on a $60 price, cost must be $30. Mixing the two fit cause serious pricing loss for small business.

Wetin if na target margin I want instead?

Work am backwards: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin ÷ 100). For 40% margin on cost of $40, price = $40 ÷ 0.60 = $66.67.

Tax suppose dey inside the cost or I go add am later?

Use the same tax basis for cost and price. This example's selling price no include sales tax; whether purchase tax belong inside your cost depend on whether you fit recover am and the local rules. Calculate the sales-tax base and rate wey apply separately.

Markup fit pass 100%?

Yes, luxury goods and goods wey cost little often get am. 200% markup go triple cost: $40 go become $120, with 66.67% margin.

This one cover discounts?

No. Discount dey come off selling price after markup. Set price with markup first, then remove discount to see the final profit.
