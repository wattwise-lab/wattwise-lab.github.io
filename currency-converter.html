Formula

Amount received = source amount × exchange rate × (1 − fee rate). Equivalently: gross = amount × rate; fee amount = gross × fee %; received = gross − fee amount.

Worked example

1,000 × 7.2 = 7,200 gross. A 1% fee is 7,200 × 0.01 = 72, so the amount received is 7,200 − 72 = 7,128. The effective rate is 7,128 ÷ 1,000 = 7.128 per unit.

Scope and limitations

This page does not provide a live or market exchange rate, and the default rate is an example only. Banks and payment providers add spreads and fixed fees that this single fee percentage may not capture; confirm the actual rate, spread, and any fixed charge before sending money. Display values are rounded to 6 decimal places; real providers round to the currency's smallest unit, which can change the final cents.

FAQ

Is the default rate the current market rate?

No. It is an example value (7.2) carried over from the original tool, clearly labelled as an example. This page fetches no market data — always replace it with a rate your provider quotes.

Does the fee cover the provider's spread?

Only if you fold the spread into the fee percentage yourself. A provider quoting a worse rate than the market is charging you a hidden spread on top of any stated fee.

What if the fee is a fixed amount, not a percentage?

Subtract it from the gross yourself: received = amount × rate − fixed fee. This page models percentage fees only.

Why is the amount received lower than amount × rate?

Because the fee percentage is deducted from the converted amount. With a 0% fee the two are equal.

Can I convert in the other direction?

Yes: divide instead of multiplying. If 1,000 units buy 7,128 at the example rate and fee, the reverse effective rate is 1,000 ÷ 7,128 = 0.140292 per unit.
