Formula

Monthly cost = calls × (input tokens × input price + output tokens × output price) ÷ 1,000,000, with prices in USD per million tokens. Cost per call = monthly cost ÷ calls.

Worked example

Input 2,500 tokens, output 800 tokens, 100,000 calls, input price $5 and output price $20 per million tokens. Input cost = 100,000 × 2,500 × 5 ÷ 1,000,000 = $1,250. Output cost = 100,000 × 800 × 20 ÷ 1,000,000 = $1,600. Total = $2,850 per month, or $0.0285 per call. The prices in this example are illustrative only — enter your own plan's prices for a usable figure.

Scope and limitations

Prices are entered per million tokens and are not live provider prices; the defaults are illustrative, not a quote for any model. Cached tokens, batch discounts, retries, failed calls, rate-limit retries, and infrastructure or staffing costs are excluded. Token counts are your averages: real calls vary, and a few long outputs can move the average materially. Taxes and currency conversion are not included.

FAQ

How is this calculated?

Each call's cost is input tokens times the input price plus output tokens times the output price, divided by 1,000,000; that per-call cost times the monthly call count is the monthly total.

Are the default prices real provider prices?

No. They are example inputs. Provider prices change and differ by model and tier, so enter the prices from your own plan or invoice before using the result.

Why is output cost often larger than input cost?

Output prices per token are usually higher than input prices, and long outputs add up. The breakdown table shows the split so you can see which side to optimise.

What is excluded?

Cached or discounted tokens, batch pricing, retries, infrastructure, and staff time. If those are material for you, add them separately rather than treating this total as a full cost of ownership.

How do I estimate tokens per call?

Measure a sample of real calls: record input and output token counts, average them, and re-check after prompts change. Averages from a handful of short test calls usually understate production calls.

Does this predict my bill exactly?

No. It is an estimate from your averages and prices. Compare it with an actual invoice for one month, then adjust the token averages or prices until the model tracks reality.
