Formula

Average acceleration a = (v₁ − v₀) ÷ t. With constant acceleration, signed displacement is s = ((v₀ + v₁) ÷ 2) × t. If velocity reverses sign, travelled distance is the sum of the distances before and after the zero-velocity time, not the absolute displacement. Negative acceleration means signed velocity decreases; speed can increase or decrease depending on direction. Enter velocity in m/s and time in seconds for acceleration in m/s² and displacement or distance in metres.

Worked example

From rest (0 m/s) to 30 m/s in 5 seconds: a = (30 − 0) ÷ 5 = 6.00 m/s². The velocity change is 30 m/s, and the distance covered is ((0 + 30) ÷ 2) × 5 = 15 × 5 = 75 m. For scale, 6 m/s² is a brisk car launch — roughly 0 to 100 km/h in about 4.6 seconds at that rate (100 km/h is 27.78 m/s, and 27.78 ÷ 6 = 4.63 s).

Scope and limitations

This page computes average acceleration over the interval, assuming it is constant. Real acceleration usually varies (an engine's pull fades as speed rises), in which case this result is the average, not the peak. It is one-dimensional and supports reversal along that axis. Curved paths, circular motion and multi-axis vector components are not covered. Time must be above zero — an instant change (t = 0) would imply infinite acceleration, which the page refuses rather than displays.

FAQ

What is the difference between velocity and acceleration?

Velocity is how fast something moves; acceleration is how fast that velocity changes. A car cruising at a steady 30 m/s has high velocity and zero acceleration; the same car speeding up from 0 to 30 m/s has positive acceleration throughout.

What does a negative acceleration mean?

Negative acceleration points along the negative axis. It reduces speed when velocity is positive, but increases speed when velocity is negative. If velocity crosses zero, the object slows, reverses direction, then speeds up.

Why is the unit m/s²?

Because acceleration is velocity change per unit time: (m/s) ÷ s = m/s². It reads as "metres per second, per second" — each second, velocity grows by that many m/s.

How does this relate to force?

Newton's second law: force = mass × acceleration. A 1,000 kg car accelerating at 6 m/s² needs 6,000 N of net force. This page does not ask for mass, so it cannot report force.

Can I use km/h instead of m/s?

Only if you convert first: divide km/h by 3.6 to get m/s, and convert time to seconds. Mixing km/h with seconds gives a number that is not m/s². The velocity calculator's conversions can help.

Is 9.8 m/s² special?

Yes — it is g, Earth's surface gravity. An object in free fall (ignoring air resistance) accelerates downward at about 9.81 m/s². Enter v₀ = 0, v₁ = 9.81, t = 1 to see it.
