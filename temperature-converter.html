Formula

Convert the source temperature to Celsius, then apply the target scale's equation.

ConversionFormula
°F to °C°C = (°F − 32) × 5/9
°C to °F°F = °C × 9/5 + 32
°C to KK = °C + 273.15
K to °C°C = K − 273.15

Absolute zero is 0 K = −273.15 °C = −459.67 °F. Any input that implies a negative Kelvin value is physically impossible and is rejected.

Worked example

25 °C × 9/5 + 32 = 77 °F, and 25 + 273.15 = 298.15 K. The scales meet at −40: −40 × 9/5 + 32 = −40 °F.

Scope and limitations

Temperature differences are not the same as absolute temperatures: a rise of 1 °C equals a rise of 1 K but a rise of 1.8 °F. This page converts absolute temperatures, not differences. Display values are rounded to 6 decimal places. Rounding may affect the final displayed decimals, as the source tool noted.

FAQ

What is absolute zero?

0 K = −273.15 °C = −459.67 °F — the coldest possible temperature. This page rejects anything colder as invalid input.

Why do °C and °F meet at −40?

Solving C = C × 9/5 + 32 gives C = −40, the only temperature with the same number on both scales.

Is 0 °C the same as 32 °F?

Yes: water freezes at 0 °C = 32 °F = 273.15 K at standard atmospheric pressure.

How do I convert a temperature difference?

For a difference, drop the +32/+273.15 offsets: 10 °C of warming = 18 °F of warming = 10 K of warming.

Why 273.15 and not 273?

273.15 is the exact offset between the Celsius and Kelvin scales by definition of the kelvin.
