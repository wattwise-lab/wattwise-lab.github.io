Formula

Energy (kWh) = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours per day × days × device count. Cost = energy × price per kWh.

Worked example

Power 1,500 W, 4 hours per day, 30 days, 1 device, rate 0.80 per kWh (an example rate you replace with your own): daily energy = 1,500 ÷ 1,000 × 4 = 6 kWh, so period energy = 6 × 30 = 180 kWh and cost = 180.00 × 0.80 = 144.00. Daily cost is 4.80. The rate here is only the example default — your bill's actual rate is what matters.

Scope and limitations

Standby power, duty cycles (appliances that cycle on and off), tiered rates, taxes, demand charges and time-of-use pricing are not modelled: a single flat rate is applied to all energy. If your appliance cycles, enter its average power draw, not its peak wattage. No rate is presented as a market average — the default rate is an example to replace.

FAQ

How does the electricity cost calculator work?

Cost = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours per day × days × device count × price per kWh. A kilowatt-hour (kWh) is 1,000 watts used for one hour.

What rate should I enter?

The per-kWh price from your own bill or tariff, including only what you want the estimate to cover. If your bill adds taxes or fixed charges separately, this estimate will not match the bill total.

What about standby power?

Standby draw is not included unless you add it: either add standby watts to the power input for the hours the device is plugged in, or run a second calculation for standby use.

My appliance cycles on and off — what power do I enter?

Its average draw over the hours you enter, not the peak wattage on the label. A fridge rated at 150 W may average far less across a day.

Why does my bill differ from this estimate?

Tiered rates, time-of-use pricing, fixed daily charges, taxes and other appliances on the same bill all sit outside this single-appliance calculation.

Does CalcMintly store my inputs?

No. This calculation runs locally in your browser and the values you enter are not sent to a CalcMintly database.
