Formula

Tax = subtotal × tax rate. Total = subtotal + tax.

Worked example

Subtotal $100, tax rate 8.25%: tax = $100 × 0.0825 = $8.25, and total = $100 + $8.25 = $108.25.

Scope and limitations

Rates, taxable items, sourcing rules, and local surtaxes vary by jurisdiction. Some items may be exempt or taxed at a different rate, and the applicable rate must be confirmed for the actual transaction — this calculator applies one rate to the whole subtotal.

FAQ

How does the sales tax calculator work?

Tax = subtotal × tax rate; total = subtotal + tax. One rate is applied to the whole subtotal you enter.

Which tax rate should I enter?

The rate that applies to the transaction — it varies by jurisdiction and sometimes by item type. Confirm it from the seller's checkout or your local tax authority; no rate is pre-assumed here.

What if some items are tax-exempt?

Enter only the taxable subtotal. Exempt items should be excluded from the subtotal, or calculated separately and added to the total afterwards.

Does this handle tax-inclusive prices?

No — it adds tax to a tax-exclusive subtotal. If your price already includes tax, the tax portion is price − price ÷ (1 + rate), which is a different calculation.

Why is the total rounded to cents?

Money totals are shown to two decimals. If your jurisdiction rounds tax per line item rather than on the subtotal, the result can differ by a cent or two.

Is this a filing or compliance calculation?

No. It is an estimate for a single transaction. Filing, registration, and sourcing rules are outside its scope.
